## What this does and doesn't show

**Vendor and operator claims (rows 1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 25, and partly 23, 24, 26, 27).** These carry most of the large numbers: Klarna's 700 agents and $40M, Rufus's "nearly $12 billion", Fin's 76%, Piper's 3.4x, Lovable's $100M ARR. None publishes a control group. Three structural problems recur:

- Self-selection. Rufus users being "60% more likely to complete a purchase" compares people who chose to ask questions with people who did not. Shoppers with purchase intent ask more questions.
- Metric definitions favor the seller. Fin counts a "resolution" when no further help is requested after the AI's last answer; abandonment and resolution look the same. Salesforce reports both "over 75%" and "more than 63%" for the same help site.
- Wrong baseline. Zalando's 23% click lift compares assistant v2 with assistant v1, not with the site without an assistant.
- Moving numbers. The Qualified Demandbase page's headline changed from "2X" pipeline to "3.4x more meetings" at the same URL.

**Independent or methodologically stated evidence (rows 15-22, 28, 29, and the incident rows 9-14).**

- Causal evidence exists, and the effects are moderate. Fang et al. (row 22) ran randomized field experiments across seven retail workflows. Effects ranged from none detectable to 16.3%, about $5 per consumer per year, and they were largest for inexperienced shoppers. Spatharioti et al. (row 20) found LLM search faster and more satisfying than ordinary search. When the model was wrong, almost half of users made the wrong decision.
- User research converges on narrow, answer-shaped utility. In NN/g's 2026 studies (rows 17-19), users ignored site chatbots, did not treat them as conversations, and went back to search and pages to verify facts that mattered, especially prices. The pre-LLM peer-reviewed comparison (row 21) found chat interfaces lowered perceived autonomy and raised cognitive load compared with menus. Autonomy predicted satisfaction.
- Stated preferences lean against AI-only service. 64% would rather companies not use AI (row 15), and 87% say human access is essential (row 16). These are surveys of service contexts, not marketing sites.
- Generative UI evidence is promising but comes from the vendor. In Google's own evaluation (row 23), generated pages ranked above top search results and markdown and below expert-built sites. The evaluation excluded latency of "a minute or two".

**What the evidence does not show.**

- No source found reports results, positive or negative, for a company that replaced its marketing homepage with a chat-first or agent-led experience. Searches for chat-first homepage launches and reversals in 2024-2026 returned opinion posts, agency marketing and product launches such as ChatGPT Atlas, not measured outcomes. The closest precedents fall into two groups:
  - Products whose homepage is the product's prompt box (Lovable, row 25). There the conversation is the deliverable.
  - Assistants embedded in large retail catalogs (Rufus, Zalando). There the chat supplements full navigation and search and does not replace them.
- No independent study found measures B2B conversational marketing (Drift, Qualified, HubSpot bots) against a control. The published B2B numbers are all vendor case studies.
- McKinsey's personalization "40 percent" is a correlation between growth and the share of revenue attributed to personalization. It is not a measured lift from adaptive pages.

## Strongest counter-evidence

1. **Users don't use on-site chatbots unless forced, and treat them as a search box (NN/g, rows 17-18).** This is the most direct evidence on the exact surface in question. Participants who were regular customers of the tested sites "had never noticed or used the chat features". Value showed up only because "we asked users to engage with the chatbot longer than they would have done on their own."
2. **A chat-led site is legally the company's own statement (Air Canada, row 10).** The tribunal said "it makes no difference whether the information comes from a static page or a chatbot." An agent that improvises claims about pricing, capabilities or policy creates liability that a reviewed static page does not. NYC MyCity, Cursor and DPD (rows 11, 13, 14) show that confident wrong answers, non-deterministic answers and on-brand embarrassment are routine failure modes, and that the usual fix was to switch the AI element off.
3. **The flagship "AI replaces humans" case partly reversed (Klarna, rows 1-2).** Within about 15 months the company publicly committed that "there will be always a human if you want". Customer surveys (rows 15-16) point the same way: 87% call human access essential.
4. **People verify consequential facts outside the chat (NN/g row 19; Spatharioti row 20).** Users don't trust AI-stated prices and confirm them via search and pages. When they don't verify, they over-rely: 60% made a single query before deciding. An agent-only site removes the verification path users currently rely on.
5. **Company chatbots are losing to third-party AI, and few report financial returns (row 16).** Customers are "roughly three times more likely" to use outside GenAI tools than a company chatbot. Company chatbot use "has been statistically flat since 2022", and only 24% of service leaders report positive financial returns. Adobe's data (row 26) shows visitors increasingly arrive already briefed by external AI. That argues for machine-readable, verifiable pages at least as strongly as for an on-site agent.
6. **Measured causal lifts are real but modest and depend on context (Fang et al., row 22).** The randomized evidence supports single-digit to mid-teens gains in specific workflows, sometimes zero, and biggest for novices. It does not support step-change claims.
7. **Operational and security exposure (Drift, row 9; Vercel RSC pause, row 24).** A widely deployed conversational-marketing widget became a credential-theft vector across "numerous corporate Salesforce instances". A leading generative-UI library was paused by its own vendor. Agent-led surfaces add supply-chain and maintenance risk that static pages do not carry.
